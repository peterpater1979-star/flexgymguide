You don't need a meal plan to enjoy your workout. A bottle of water, something small to eat at the right time and a snack for afterwards will get you a long way. This guide covers what many people bring, and what you can safely skip.

<p class="klein">This guide gives practical tips and is not medical or nutrition advice. If you have a condition, an allergy or a special diet, talk to a doctor or dietitian.</p>

<h2 id="drinking">Drinking: water is enough</h2>

For a one-hour class or a normal gym session, water is usually all you need. Bring your own bottle: most studios and gyms let you refill it.

- **Have a few sips before you start**, especially if you haven't drunk much all day.
- **Take small sips during the class**, especially at spinning, HIIT or hot yoga.
- **Sports drinks** are for long or very hard sessions. For a normal class, water is fine.

<h2 id="before">Before your workout: small and light</h2>

If you'd rather not train on an empty stomach, eat something small an hour to an hour and a half before. A full stomach during burpees or a spin class is no fun.

- A banana or other piece of fruit
- A slice of bread or a cracker with a topping
- A pot of yoghurt
- A cereal or granola bar

Training early in the morning? Many people find half a banana or a few sips of juice enough.

<h2 id="after">After your workout: just a good meal</h2>

After training, a normal meal is often all you need. If you won't be eating soon, a snack on the go is handy:

- Yoghurt or skyr with fruit
- A handful of nuts
- A sandwich with cheese, egg or hummus
- A protein bar or shake, if you find that convenient

<h2 id="protein">Protein powder and bars: handy, not essential</h2>

Protein powder and protein bars are mainly useful when you're short on time after your workout. Mix the powder with water or milk in a shaker and you have something ready in your bag. You don't need it: everyday foods like yoghurt, eggs, beans or meat also contain protein.

When choosing one, look at:
- **The ingredients list.** Shorter is better, and check for added sugar.
- **Taste and how well it mixes.** Start with a small pack before buying a big bag.
- **Vegan or lactose-free?** There are also pea or soy-based options.

<h2 id="bag">A short list for your bag</h2>

1. A full water bottle
2. A small snack for afterwards (bar, fruit or nuts)
3. A shaker with pre-measured powder, if you use it: add water after your workout

For what to bring to yoga, boxing or spinning, read [Your first class](/guides/first-class/).

## Frequently asked questions

<details open>
<summary>Should I eat before a workout?</summary>

You don't have to. Some people train fine on an empty stomach, others feel weak. If that's you, eat something small an hour before, like a banana or a slice of bread.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do I need sports drinks?</summary>

For a normal one-hour class, usually not. Water is enough. Sports drinks are mainly for long or very intense sessions.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do I need protein powder?</summary>

No. It's mainly convenient when you have little time after your workout. You also get protein from everyday food.

</details>

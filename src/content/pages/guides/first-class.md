A first class at a new studio can feel a bit daunting, especially when you're trying a fitness pass or a free trial and don't know the place yet. With the right things in your bag and a few simple habits, your first visit is a lot easier.

<h2 id="basics">The basics for every visit</h2>

- **Comfortable clothes** that move with you and let sweat through, such as leggings or shorts and a breathable top
- **Shoes for the activity**, or clean indoor shoes if the studio asks for them
- **A water bottle**, since not every studio sells drinks
- **A small towel** for the gym floor or a sweaty class
- **A padlock** if the lockers don't have their own lock
- **Your phone** with the booking or membership in the app

<h2 id="activity">What to bring per activity</h2>

<div class="kolommen">

<div class="kolom kaart">

### Yoga and pilates

Fitted clothes that don't ride up when you bend. You usually train barefoot or in grip socks. Many studios have mats, but check this when you book.

</div>

<div class="kolom kaart">

### Boxing and kickboxing

Training shoes with a flat sole, or barefoot if the studio asks. Hand wraps and gloves: some studios rent them out, others expect you to bring your own.

</div>

<div class="kolom kaart">

### Indoor cycling

A breathable top and shorts or leggings that don't catch on the saddle. Normal training shoes are fine; cycling shoes are a bonus if the bikes have clip-in pedals.

</div>

<div class="kolom kaart">

### Strength training

Shoes with a flat, firm sole for squats and lifts. A towel to put on benches, and clothes that let you see your posture.

</div>

</div>

<h2 id="tips">Five tips for your first visit</h2>

1. **Arrive ten minutes early.** You need time to find the changing room or get a short introduction. At the entrance you check in: at the front desk, where a staff member marks you present, on a screen, or with a QR code you received by email.
2. **Tell the instructor you're new.** They can show you the basics and suggest easier versions of exercises.
3. **Mention injuries.** A sore knee or back is good for the instructor to know before you start.
4. **Start at your own level.** Nobody expects you to keep up with the regulars in your first class.
5. **Check the cancellation rules.** With a fitness pass or a trial, a late cancellation can cost credits or a fee.

## Frequently asked questions

<details open>
<summary>Do I need special sports clothes?</summary>

No. Comfortable clothes you can move and sweat in are enough for a first class. If you keep going, it's worth investing in good shoes for the activity you do most.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I shower at the studio?</summary>

Most gyms and many studios have showers, but not all. Check the studio page in the app or on their website before you go.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What if I can't keep up?</summary>

That's normal in a first class. Take a break when you need one and do an easier version of the exercise. Instructors are used to beginners.

</details>

As a student you want to work out without spending much and without being tied down for long. Your timetable changes every term, you may be away in summer and you have little time during exams. These are your options.

<h2 id="kort">In short</h2>

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Your university's sports centre** | Often the cheapest option, with classes and a gym |
| **Budget gym** | Lots of locations and long opening hours; check the contract |
| **ClassPass** | Lots of variety, but no student discount; start small |
| **Working out outdoors** | Running, cycling or an outdoor calisthenics park: free |

<h2 id="instelling">Your university or college sports centre</h2>

Many universities and colleges have their own sports centre or sports programme. As a student you often pay a low price per year or per semester for group classes, a gym and often team sports too. Check your university's website to see what's on offer. The downside: you're tied to the locations and times of that one centre.

<h2 id="budget">Budget gym</h2>

A budget gym is often cheap per month and has lots of locations. Do check the contract, though: some have a minimum term or a notice period. If you train three times a week or more in the same place, a regular membership often works out cheaper. Also read [Gym pass or membership?](/guides/gym-pass-or-membership/)

<h2 id="classpass">ClassPass</h2>

ClassPass has no student discount. It can still suit you if you want variety: yoga one week, spinning or boxing the next. Here's how to keep it affordable:

- **Start with the trial.** You'll find out for free which studios you like. Read [ClassPass free trial](/guides/classpass-free-trial/).
- **Then choose a small plan.** Plans differ by city; you'll see the smallest one in the app.
- **Train off-peak.** Daytime classes often cost fewer credits than evening ones.
- **Cancel or pause over the summer.** Your plan runs month to month, so you're not tied down for long.

<h2 id="gratis">Working out for free</h2>

Running, cycling, an outdoor calisthenics park or a yoga video at home cost nothing. Many cities also have free outdoor fitness spots. Handy in between, or when you don't want a membership for a while.

<h2 id="kiezen">What suits you?</h2>

- **You want it as cheap as possible and train often:** your university's sports centre or a budget gym.
- **You want variety and train a few times a month:** a small ClassPass plan.
- **You're often away or have a changing timetable:** something without a long contract, like ClassPass.
- **The best of both:** your university gym for regular training, plus the occasional ClassPass class you can't get there.

<h2 id="tips">Tips</h2>

1. **Ask about a student discount.** Some gyms have one, often when you show your student card.
2. **Check the notice period,** especially if you're away over the summer.
3. **Train together.** A regular session with a friend makes it easier to keep going.
4. **Don't buy more than you use.** Start small; you can always go bigger.

## Frequently asked questions

<details open>
<summary>Does ClassPass have a student discount?</summary>

No. ClassPass has no student discount. If you have a part-time job, check whether your employer offers ClassPass through a corporate plan.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What's the cheapest way to work out as a student?</summary>

Working out outdoors is free. After that, your university's sports centre is usually the cheapest option.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I stop ClassPass over the summer?</summary>

Yes. You can cancel every month, and when you cancel ClassPass often offers a pause. Read [Cancel or pause ClassPass](/guides/cancel-or-pause-classpass/).

</details>

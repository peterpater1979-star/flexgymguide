There are two ways to train without being tied to one small studio: a fitness pass that works at many studios, or a membership at a gym chain with clubs across the city. Both can be the right choice. It mostly depends on how often you train and how much variety you want.

<h2 id="short">In short</h2>

| | Fitness pass | Gym membership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Example** | [ClassPass](/guides/how-classpass-works/) | [Fitness First](/uk/fitness-first/), [The Gym Group](/uk/the-gym-group/), [Trainmore](/nl/trainmore/) |
| **How it works** | Monthly credits for classes and gym visits at many studios | Unlimited visits to the clubs of one chain |
| **Variety** | Yoga, pilates, boxing, cycling, strength and more, at different places | The classes and equipment of that chain |
| **Good if you train** | One to three times a week | Three times a week or more |
| **Abroad** | <span class="ja" aria-hidden="true">✓</span> Works in 31 countries | Usually only in one country |
| **Try it first** | Free trial | Free trial session or guest pass at most chains |

<p class="klein">Prices and terms change regularly. Always check the latest details with the provider.</p>

<h2 id="pass">How a fitness pass works</h2>

With a fitness pass you pay a monthly fee and get credits. You use those credits to book classes and gym visits at studios that take part. One week you go to yoga, the next to a boxing class across town.

- Good if you like variety or don't know yet what you enjoy
- Good if you train one to three times a week
- Good if you travel or live between two cities
- Popular classes at peak times cost more credits

[How ClassPass works](/guides/how-classpass-works/)

<h2 id="membership">How a gym membership works</h2>

With a membership at a gym chain you pay a fixed monthly fee and can go as often as you like. Depending on your plan, you can use one club or all clubs of the chain. Many chains also offer group classes and, at some clubs, a sauna.

- Good if you train three times a week or more
- Good if you like a fixed routine in one place
- Often cheaper per visit if you go often
- Check the minimum term and notice period before you sign

<h2 id="choose">Three questions that help you choose</h2>

<div class="kolommen">

<div class="kolom kaart">

### How often do you train?

Up to three times a week: a pass is often the better fit. More often: a membership usually works out cheaper.

</div>

<div class="kolom kaart">

### How much variety do you want?

Want to try yoga, boxing and cycling? A pass. Mostly strength and cardio? A membership.

</div>

<div class="kolom kaart">

### Do you travel a lot?

A pass also works in other countries. Most gym chains only work in their own country.

</div>

</div>

<h2 id="both">Can you combine them?</h2>

Yes. Some people have a cheap gym membership for strength training and use a fitness pass for classes now and then, or only while travelling. Just check that the total still fits your budget.

## Frequently asked questions

<details open>
<summary>Is a fitness pass cheaper than a gym?</summary>

That depends on how often you go. If you train once or twice a week, a pass often costs less than a membership plus separate classes. If you go to the same gym three times a week or more, a membership is usually cheaper.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I try both first?</summary>

Yes. ClassPass has a free trial, and most gym chains offer a free trial session or guest pass. Trying both for a week tells you more than any price list.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I cancel at any time?</summary>

You can change or cancel a ClassPass plan before your next renewal date. Gym memberships often have a minimum term and a notice period, so check this before you sign.

</details>
